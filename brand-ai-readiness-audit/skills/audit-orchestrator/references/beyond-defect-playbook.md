# Recommendations beyond the defects found

A site can pass every mechanical check and still be almost impossible to cite, because
nothing on it is worth quoting and nothing outside it corroborates what it says. These
recommendations are emitted regardless of findings, gated on what the site already has so
they stay specific.

## 1. A canonical facts page, mirrored everywhere

One page holding the legal name, founding year, headquarters, leadership, one-sentence
description and key numbers with dates. Then copy that exact wording into every external
profile. Agreement across independent sources is the mechanism by which a claim becomes
repeatable — and agreement means the same words, not the same gist.

## 2. Question-shaped content

Assistants match a user's question to a passage. Content organised around themes matches
nothing. Mine sales calls, support tickets and site-search logs for the real questions;
answer each in the first sentence, in a passage that stands alone without the heading.

## 3. Comparison and alternatives pages you control

Comparison questions are where assistants pick winners, and they are usually answered from
third-party listicles written by people with no stake in accuracy. An honest, specific,
dated comparison on the brand's own domain is a citable source for exactly those queries.
Being accurate about where a competitor is stronger is what earns the citation — a
one-sided page is discounted by both readers and machines.

## 4. Original data

The most durable way to be repeated by machines is to be repeated by people. Original
benchmarks, survey results or operational data get linked and quoted, which puts the brand
name on domains it does not control. That is the corroboration stage 5 is measuring, and
it cannot be bought with on-site changes.

## 5. Direct measurement

Every check in this audit is a proxy. The real measure is what assistants say when asked.
Write 20 prompts covering the brand name, its category, its competitors and its top buying
questions. Run them monthly across assistants. Record the answers, the sources cited, and
any factual errors. Track the share of answers citing the brand's own domain.

Segment AI-referral traffic separately in analytics: it lands deep, converts differently,
and is invisible if bucketed with search.

## 6. Email that survives summarisation

Inboxes increasingly show a generated summary instead of the message. Substance carried
only in images, or buried under filler, never reaches the summary. Put the point in the
first two lines as text, never carry the offer or date only in a graphic, and keep a
`text/plain` part in every campaign.

## 7. A stable, quotable vocabulary

Consistent naming is what lets separate mentions be recognised as the same thing. A product
renamed across pages fragments into several apparent products. Fix one canonical name and
one description per product, use them verbatim everywhere, and publish a glossary for terms
the brand uses in its own way.

## 8. llms.txt, with realistic expectations

A plain-text index of the pages worth citing costs an hour. Adoption is partial and it is
not a substitute for the HTML being readable, so recommend it as cheap insurance and say so
plainly. Claiming it fixes discoverability is the kind of overreach that makes a whole
report suspect.
